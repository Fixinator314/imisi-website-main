02 / Proposed pilot programmes

Curiosity needs
somewhere to go.

Six connected opportunities put the Heritage Pipeline into practice. Start with an encounter, then explore the support that could help a learner continue.

In development · Proposed Thulamela pilot
01 / Museum02 / Cohort03 / Experience04 / Create05 / Pathways06 / Return
01Discover · ConnectTravelling MuseumAn encounter with Thulamela through material culture, visual stories and practical activities.+

The proposed trailer combines display drawers, information panels and interactive learning. Replicas and artisan contributions connect archaeological evidence with living practice.

Proposed format
Designed for park visitors, surrounding schools and communities.
The next step
An invitation to choose an interest and continue learning.
02LearnHeritage Explorer CohortA proposed community learning programme shaped around learners’ curiosity.+

A local youth leader provides continuity while specialists join in person or remotely. The proposed eight-session structure moves through evidence, living knowledge, material exploration, careers, practical skills and an individual next-step conversation.

Proposed format
Community sessions with a mix of physical, digital and hybrid activities.
The next step
A small project and a choice of deeper experience.
03ExperienceField visits & professional shadowingA closer look at what heritage work feels like in practice.+

Learners could visit a heritage site, observe appropriate fieldwork, spend time in an artisan workshop or explore a museum or research environment. Access is arranged around interests, available hosts and practical requirements.

Proposed format
Visits and observation matched to each learner’s interests.
The next step
A clearer understanding of the work they want to explore.
04CreateContemporary Material TransformationAn opportunity to contribute something of their own.+

Learners draw on archaeological context and living practice to develop a contemporary response. This could be an object, exhibition, research project, film, photograph or oral history.

Proposed format
A guided project with relevant practitioner feedback.
The next step
Work that expresses the learner’s own questions and ideas.
05TransitionIndividual Pathway SupportPractical guidance towards a meaningful next step.+

A personal pathway plan records interests, experience and a next goal. Relevant introductions and guidance could connect learners with further study, apprenticeships, mentors or other available opportunities.

Proposed format
Individual conversations, referrals and application guidance.
The next step
A specific next action, contact and follow-up date.
06ReturnYouth Heritage AmbassadorsA way to give back while continuing to learn.+

Participants could support a future museum visit, demonstrate a skill, help younger learners or share their experience. Over time, this involvement could grow into facilitation, mentorship or professional contribution.

Proposed format
Peer support and community participation.
The next step
Another learner’s starting point.

Different interests.
Different journeys.

The museum is an entry point. Learners who want to go further can choose a supported pathway; their interests help determine the next step. Pilot formats and opportunities will be tested and refined with partners.

03 / Inside the learning cohort

An interest.
A direction.

A proposed eight-session Heritage Explorer Cohort gives interested learners time to investigate, try skills and choose what they want to experience next.

Pilot structure · In development

0102030405060708

A local youth leader anchors the cohort, with specialists joining in person or remotely. The schedule and session formats will be tested with communities and partners.

Session 01 / Reflect

What stayed with me?

Learners revisit the museum encounter and map their interests: an object, a person, a skill or an unanswered question.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
An initial interest profile
Proposed format
In person preferred

Session 02 / Investigate

How do we know?

A researcher introduces archaeological evidence through a practical case. Learners compare materials, consider context and explore what evidence can and cannot tell us.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
Practice asking questions with evidence
Proposed format
Hybrid

Session 03 / Meet

Who carries knowledge today?

An artisan or community practitioner shares how they learned their craft, how they choose materials and what experience teaches them.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
A connection with living knowledge
Proposed format
In person preferred

Session 04 / Connect

One material, many worlds

Beads, bone and clay connect archaeological questions with scientific analysis, living practice and contemporary design. Learners identify which part interests them.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
A broader view of possible pathways
Proposed format
Hybrid

Session 05 / Explore

Who does this work?

Practitioners discuss their working lives, how they entered their fields and what a young person could do next. Learners can ask questions that connect to their own interests.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
A clearer picture of heritage careers
Proposed format
Digital or hybrid

Session 06 / Try

What do I enjoy doing?

A practical skills lab lets learners try observation and classification, making, museum interpretation and media storytelling.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
Experience of different skills
Proposed format
In person

Session 07 / Choose

What do I want to explore?

Each learner pursues a small challenge in an area they have chosen, with a relevant mentor conversation and feedback.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
A mini-project and focused guidance
Proposed format
Hybrid

Session 08 / Continue

What is my next step?

Learners share their work with the community, reflect on their interests and discuss an appropriate field visit, shadowing opportunity or other deeper experience.

Inside this session +
What learners take forward
An individual next-step conversation
Proposed format
In person

The cohort ends with a next step.
